The short version

The foundations are strong. Colour contrast is guaranteed by the theme tokens across all 8 palettes in light and dark. Almost nothing in 53,000 lines bypasses the tokens. One engine produces every Safe Spending figure.
The problem is composition. Every section is the same bordered card. A loaded Home stacks 12 to 18 of them over about five screens, so after the big number nothing leads the eye.
Safe Spending tells only part of its story. The hero shows the amount. Days left, spending pace and upcoming bills sit in three other cards further down, and the same facts are repeated up to five times.
There is no typographic identity. The app uses the system font, has no styles for money, and formats amounts three different ways.
The audit found 8 bugs. Five can be fixed in the UI during the redesign. Three sit in the data or domain layer and need your approval.
The plan stays inside the UI. Every change to Home uses data the screen already receives. Two signature ideas need small domain additions, listed below for approval.

Decisions for you before Phase 2

Status: all six recommendations accepted on 8 Oct 2026. Phases 2 (design system), 3 (Home), 4 (Expenses), 5 (Budgets and Bills) and 6 (Reports) are built on them; Phase 7 (Settings) is next.

1

Typeface

Recommended: bundle Manrope, one small variable font file that works offline, for all text and numbers, with tabular figures on every amount.

It has true tabular digits, a real minus sign and crisp numerals at hero size, and it reads more distinctive than Inter. Compare all three on the same content in the type specimen. Inter is the safest small-text choice; Plus Jakarta Sans is the warmest.

2

Navigation icons

Recommended: replace the Rive icons with Material Symbols that cross-fade from outlined to filled.

The Rive Expenses clipboard is detailed and skeuomorphic next to three simple line icons, and the Rive file's licence is still unconfirmed in TODO.md. The bar's public API, keys and tests stay the same; the existing Material renderer is already used in tests.

3

Colour for "Over today's amount"

Recommended: use caution amber instead of red.

Going over today's amount is recoverable, because tomorrow's figure absorbs it. Red stays for money that is already gone: over budget, or bills exceeding what is left. One hero test that pins the error colour changes on purpose.

4

Dark mode for the default palette

Recommended: near-neutral dark surfaces with a slight cool bias, instead of navy.

Today every dark surface is navy, insight cards use a second brown-grey family, and charts fight the blue. Monarch retired its navy dark theme for the same reason. The other 7 palettes keep their character.

5

Three bug fixes outside the UI layer

Recommended: fix all three, on this branch or a separate fix branch.

(a) Home's recent expenses all show "12:00 AM" because the dashboard datasource passes the date column, not the time. (b) On a fresh install the evening reminder defaults to 9:00 AM, because the settings datasource falls back to 9:00 for every time instead of the 8:00 PM default. (c) Reports shows ₹ for any period outside the current month, because the report data carries no currency.

6

Two small domain additions for the signature Home

Recommended: approve both for Phase 3. Defer the rest.

(a) A pure "tomorrow" function on the safe-spending calculator, so Home can say "Spend nothing more today and tomorrow's amount is ₹880" or, after an overspend, "This lowers each of the next 23 days by ₹12". Only the domain may divide by remaining days, so this cannot live in a widget. (b) A per-day spending series for the active budget, excluding bill payments, to draw a pace chart. Deferred: streaks, "vs your usual" and category-anomaly insights. They need new rules, and streaks need a schema change.

Naming. The research found that "Safe-to-Spend" was a registered US trademark of Simple, and its current status could not be verified. Monivo's in-app label, "Today's Safe Spending", is already distinct. The plan keeps it. This is not legal advice.
Emulator. The screenshot pass found the emulator's font scale at 2.0 and set it back to 1.0. If 2.0 was deliberate, it can be restored with adb shell settings put system font_scale 2.0.

What the best finance apps get right

Seven research passes read help centres, design blogs, production stylesheets and reviews, with 318 sources in total. The goal was principles, not layouts to copy.

Copilot Money

  • One "Free to Spend" headline with a one-sentence definition, explained by the chart beneath it.
  • Status colour comes from pace, not percent used, with published thresholds.
  • Ideal pace is a dotted line, actual spend a solid line ending at today. Committed bills are drawn as outlined bars.
  • Secondary amounts drop to a muted colour, never to green.
Avoid: letting rent land on the pace line, which Copilot's own FAQ admits causes false spikes.

Monarch

  • Progress bars carry a tick for where you are in the period, so 60% on day 3 and on day 28 look different.
  • A neutral middle state: exactly on plan is grey, not a celebration and not an alarm.
  • Every chart is also a filter; tapping a slice shows the transactions behind it.
  • One stat anatomy everywhere: small label, medium-weight value, quieter unit.
Avoid: charts with no headline. Reviewers called them "accurate but unreadable".

YNAB

  • The headline number is an instruction, with a small closed set of states and one verb each.
  • Absence is the success state: alerts appear only when there is something to do.
  • Red means money is already gone. Recoverable gaps are amber, and zero is grey.
  • Forgiving copy at the moment of failure: "You're human. Adjust."
Avoid: a permanent "All good!" card, which only weakens real alerts.

Monzo, Revolut, Cash App

  • The hero is already net of bills, and a muted line says what was taken out.
  • The breakdown is always one tap away.
  • Ordinary spending is neutral ink with no minus sign. Only refunds get a "+" and colour.
  • Quiet success: an icon morph and one light haptic, never a success screen.
Avoid: two hero-sized numbers side by side. Monzo had to add an option to hide its second one.

Daily-allowance apps (Simple, Toshl, Daily Budget)

  • The daily figure is large; the period total is the context underneath.
  • The number is fixed for the day and the app says so.
  • An overspend is spread across the remaining days: "lowers each of the next 12 days by ₹2".
  • At zero, show a way back, not a red negative.
Avoid: labels without an object or a date, like "8 days". Days for what?

Craft references (Apple, Material, NN/g)

  • Tabular figures on every amount, and a true minus sign (U+2212).
  • No skeleton for waits under about 300 ms. Monivo reads a local database, so most screens should never show one.
  • Sheets for short tasks, one at a time, with a grabber and a discard check when dirty.
  • Haptics with fixed meanings, used sparingly.
Avoid: counting the hero number up from zero on every open. It shows amounts that are not true.

Monivo's opening

  • Competitors answer "how is my month going?" Monivo answers "what can I spend today?", for any date range, with bills already set aside. That daily decision is the product.
  • The formula is deterministic, so Monivo can show the future exactly. No other app tells you precisely how today's choice changes tomorrow's number (decision 6).
  • Offline with no account means no waiting. Local reads finish in milliseconds, so the app can open straight to the number with no spinner or skeleton.
  • Honest states are already modelled. "Not set" differs from ₹0, and "Unavailable" differs from "none". The design can make that honesty visible instead of burying it in a ledger.

Design principles

One number leadsOnly Today's Safe Spending is set at hero size anywhere in the app. Every other figure on Home explains it.
Result first, reasoning on requestAmount, then one line of context, then a tap for the full working, laid out like a receipt.
Each fact appears onceRemaining, end date, pace and percent used each get one home per screen.
Status is a word and an iconNumbers stay in ink. Colour marks a state, never decoration, and it never contradicts the chip.
Calm by defaultAmber means slow down. Red only means money is already gone. Zero is neutral, and every bad state shows a way back.
Absence is successNotices, alerts and insights appear only when there is something to know or do.
Estimates look like estimatesProjections get "≈", whole units and a muted tone. Recorded figures stay exact.
Surfaces before cardsSections sit on the page, separated by space and type. One raised surface marks the hero. Cards are for objects you can tap.

The breakdown today is a card that is always open, about 500 dp tall, with rows for things that are not set. In the proposal it becomes a sheet with the same figures in the order of the formula, signs in their own column and amounts right-aligned in tabular figures.

  • Tabular figures live in the money roles, so no widget has to remember them. Today they are added by hand at 37 places.

  • Every status bar takes the tone of its status. Budget status comes from the engine's enums, which removes the 5 places where widgets re-derive the 80% and 100% thresholds.
  • Charts get their own tokens: up to 5 category series plus a neutral "Other", an ink trend line, a dashed pace line and a tooltip surface that follows the theme.

Shape, space and motion

  • Three radii. 20 for the hero and sheets, 14 for summary rows and object cards, 10 for fields and buttons, plus pill chips. Today cards use 24 with 16, 12 and 8 mixed in.
  • Rhythm. 16 dp gutters, 28 dp between sections, 56 dp list rows with 48 dp touch targets.
  • Motion. Keep the existing tokens and reduced-motion handling, aligned to Material 3 durations. Numbers change with a short cross-fade only after the user acts, never on open. Charts reveal once per data change.
  • Reduced motion on iOS. The app reads only the disableAnimations flag. Phase 2 checks on an iPhone whether iOS Reduce Motion reaches it, and adds the iOS flag if not.
  • Haptics in one place. Selection click for chips and periods, a light impact on save, one medium impact when a swipe crosses the delete threshold, nothing on scroll or keys.

Architecture findings

  • Clean Architecture holds. Each feature has domain, data and presentation layers. BLoCs barely compute. All safe-spending maths sits in two pure classes with documented rules and worked-example tests.
  • Routing is solid. Four preloaded tabs in a stateful shell with a custom fade-through container whose structure must not change. Forms and details open on the root navigator. Duplicate pushes are blocked.
  • The lock gate keeps the app mounted, so unlocking never replays loading. The home-screen widget and notifications read the same engine as the dashboard.
  • Presentation re-derives budget status in five places: the progress colour helper, the Reports utilisation card, the budget card, budget details and a legacy target in the dashboard BLoC. The redesign reads the domain's status instead.
  • Smart insights are an id, a message and a type. The amount is baked into the message text, so the UI can filter and order insights but cannot style their numbers. Structured insights would need an entity change, which is deferred.
  • Provider is installed but effectively unused. The one provided value is read through GetIt everywhere. Nothing in the plan touches it.
  • Tests pin 53 keys and about 180 visible strings across 208 widget tests. Each phase below names the tests it changes on purpose. There are no golden or screenshot tests, so Phase 2 adds them.

Experience

  • The Safe Spending story is scattered. The amount is in the hero, days left in a card two screens down, pace inside the breakdown about 500 dp below, and bills behind a collapsed row.
  • Facts repeat. Remaining, end date, pace and percent used appear up to five times on Home, and two different "short" figures disagree.
  • Adding an expense feels like a form. 13 category chips in catalogue order push date and note below the fold. Validation fires on every keystroke. Saving holds for 450 ms. Closing after changes discards them without asking.
  • Expenses spends 43% of the screen on controls before the first row, including a permanent switch for Combined view, which few people need.
  • Reports repeats itself and cannot be explored. Four of seven insights restate the caption beside them. The period chips and the filter sheet's dates can disagree. Nothing drills down. An error replaces the whole screen with raw exception text.
  • Settings is one long list of equal boxes. Seven row styles, "CSV" and "JSON" as button labels, a Restore button that looks as harmless as Export, and 24-hour time pills beside 12-hour text.
  • Insight copy reads like logs or filler. "Did you know", "Keep it up!" and "Personal: you've spent…". Alerts about other budgets outrank the active one.
  • Raw exception text reaches people in at least 16 messages.

Phase plan

Phase 2Design system

  • Bundle the chosen typeface. Rebuild the text theme with six text roles and five money roles, tracking tuned to the face.
  • Add tokens for status containers, raised and sunken surfaces and chart series, all through the existing contrast engine, theme animation and contrast tests.
  • Settle the three radii, borderless cards by default and the near-neutral dark surfaces if approved.
  • Build the components above and a status-to-tone helper fed by the engine's enums. Align motion to Material 3 and put haptics in one place.
  • Add a golden-test harness: light and dark, 360 dp wide, 2.0 text scale.
Visible change: the new type and colour app-wide, layouts untouched. Tests extended: theme contrast, plus new tests for money formatting and status tones.

Phase 3Home

  • Header with the budget as title and switcher, the hero as in the concept, Free to spend as one row opening the ledger sheet.
  • Notices as one quiet row, bills as "Coming up", other budgets and recent expenses as flat lists, no quick actions.
  • Insights shown only when they add something: duplicates of the hero, pace and progress are filtered by id, and warnings come first.
  • The FAB shrinks to an icon while scrolling and the list keeps clear of it. A skeleton that matches the new layout, shown only for slow loads.
  • The "tomorrow" line and pace chart, if decision 6 is approved.
Tests changed on purpose: screen ordering and the repeated "Remaining in budget", the two "Not set" rows, the background the accessibility test assumes for the hero, and the "Over by" colour if decision 3 is approved. Keys stay.

Phase 4Expenses

  • A header that scrolls away; Combined view moves into the budget menu; the summary becomes one line of type.
  • 56 dp rows shared with Home, day headers with "Spent ₹570" in muted text.
  • Quick add as a sheet: an amount pad, your five most used categories, Today and Yesterday chips, and "More details" for budget, time, tags and receipt. The full form stays for edit and duplicate.
  • A discard check, quiet success, validation on save rather than per keystroke, and details as one surface with undoable delete.
Tests changed on purpose: the spinner inside the category picker. "Summary", "2 items", sort labels and empty-state strings stay.

Phase 5Budgets and Bills

  • The active budget becomes the one raised card, with "Home, Expenses and Reports follow this budget". Others become compact rows with a period tick.
  • Total remaining becomes a quiet per-currency footer, so nothing looks pooled.
  • Budget details without a card grid. "Add expense" only on the active budget, otherwise "Make active".
  • Bills grouped as Overdue, Due soon and Later with per-currency totals, a single pay sheet and compact rows. Both forms get progressive disclosure.
Tests changed on purpose: none expected. Pay-flow buttons and dialog text are kept exactly.

Phase 6Reports

  • A "This budget" period alongside the existing ones, and one place to choose dates.
  • An answer-first total with a comparison sentence; categories as ranked bars, tap to see those expenses; daily columns with an average line and tap-to-inspect.
  • Planned versus actual with a period tick, a weekday rhythm chart, insights only where they add something, export moved to the menu.
  • An error keeps the last report on screen with a retry.
Tests: no screen-level test exists, so this phase adds them. The category comparison widget's pins stay.

Phase 7Settings

  • A summary strip of current choices, then grouped lists without borders and one row anatomy.
  • Theme as a segmented control, palettes previewed as small screens in light and dark.
  • Data actions open sheets that explain each format. Restore is styled as destructive. One time format.
  • Categories as a dense list; the converter as one surface.
Tests changed on purpose: none expected. The tile, switch and selector structures tests rely on are kept.

Phase 8Polish

  • Onboarding: currency before amount, and the first day's Safe Spending as the hero of the last step.
  • A lock screen without a spinner, an empty and error copy sweep, and skeleton timing.
  • A motion and haptics audit, large text at 1.3 and 2.0, and a two-column Home on tablets.
  • Goldens complete for every screen, and device QA in light and dark.

Quality gates for every screen

  • Format only the files that changed. Running the formatter over all of lib rewrites an unrelated file.
  • flutter analyze clean, and the full test suite passing, run a second time with TZ=America/New_York to catch date bugs.
  • Golden tests in light and dark at 360 dp and 2.0 text scale. Flutter's tap-target, label and contrast guideline tests on the screen.
  • Screenshots on the emulator in light, dark, 1.3 and 2.0 text, plus the tablet emulator for wide layouts.
  • Loading, empty and error states forced in widget tests, not just the happy path.
  • A diff check that nothing under domain/ or data/ changed unless it was approved above.
