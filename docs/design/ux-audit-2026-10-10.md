## A. Executive summary

### Overall assessment

Monivo's presentation layer is unusually mature for a personal app. It has a real token system with contrast floors built into the theme. Money formatting floors "safe" amounts and never pools currencies. Most screens have deliberate screen-reader labels, reduced motion is respected almost everywhere, and the Safe-to-Spend engine has one implementation with a clear "how it's worked out" explanation. Visually, the app reads as calm and premium.

The weaknesses are about **trust**, not looks. In a few places the app shows a figure that is out of date, labelled in the wrong currency, or missing something the user needs to know (an overdue bill, a blocked reminder, a backup that can't be reached). There is also friction at the budget-period boundary, which every user meets once a period. These problems are concentrated, specific and mostly small to fix.

**Production readiness:** not yet, from a UX and trust point of view. The 12 P0 items (section G) are the minimum before a wider release. With P0 and P1 fixed, the core journeys are in good shape.

**UX maturity (provisional):** 3.5 / 5. Design-system discipline is at 4–4.5. State and lifecycle handling (midnight, period end, permissions, data portability) is at about 2.5.

### Strengths worth preserving

1. **One Safe-to-Spend number, explained.** The hero gives the amount, a status word and icon, today's spend against it, and one sentence on why. "How it's worked out" is a receipt-style breakdown that ends at the hero figure. The engine is the single source (`SafeToSpendCalculator`), and safe amounts are floored for display.
2. **Honest tones.** Red is reserved for money already gone. Going over today's amount is caution amber, because tomorrow absorbs it. Status is never shown by colour alone.
3. **Currency hygiene.** Totals are per currency and never added across currencies (Budgets footer, Bills groups, combined Expenses view). Decimal input follows each currency's minor units.
4. **Design-system discipline.** Dialogs, sheets, `AnimatedSize` and navigation all go through wrappers (0 raw `showDialog`, 0 raw `context.push`). There are only 5 literal colours outside the theme, and contrast is enforced in the tokens.
5. **Fast capture.** Quick add takes 6 taps for a typical expense, ranks categories by use, puts the amount in the button ("Add ₹250"), and has large keypad keys.
6. **Recoverable deletes.** Swipe to delete with no dialog, plus a 6-second Undo that restores the same row.
7. **Accessibility groundwork.** Composite semantic labels on the hero, breakdown, bill rows and rankings; `liveRegion` on errors; and guideline tests for the nav bar, hero and several screens.
8. **Test safety net.** About 151 test files, golden coverage of 19 screens at 1× and 2× text, and integration tests against in-memory Drift.

### The five biggest problems

1. **Home can show yesterday's numbers.** Nothing refreshes Home, or the widget, when the app resumes or the date changes ([UX-001](#ux-001)). The morning check, the app's main use, can read yesterday's "Today's Safe Spending" and "Spent today".
2. **Data safety is weaker than the Settings screen implies.** "Back up" writes a file the user can't reach or take off the device ([UX-002](#ux-002)). The only file that can leave the device is labelled a backup, but Restore rejects it ([UX-003](#ux-003)). Re-importing duplicates expenses ([UX-008](#ux-008)), and importing a tagged export fails ([UX-009](#ux-009)).
3. **Currency integrity has silent gaps.** Changing a budget's currency relabels every existing expense ([UX-004](#ux-004)), and moving an expense to a budget in another currency keeps the number ([UX-005](#ux-005)). The combined view and the PDF can print the wrong symbol ([UX-007](#ux-007), [UX-010](#ux-010)). The currency chosen during onboarding is never saved as the default ([UX-016](#ux-016)).
4. **Friction at the budget-period boundary.** Creating next month's budget immediately makes it active, so Home loses today's figure ([UX-015](#ux-015)). There is no "start next period" from a budget ([UX-022](#ux-022)), and an ended budget is still shown as a green "Active" ([UX-021](#ux-021)). Quick add fails only after "Add" is tapped ([UX-020](#ux-020)), and Reports' calendar periods look empty for earlier budgets ([UX-017](#ux-017)).
5. **Features that fail without saying so.** Notifications show as "on" when the OS has blocked them ([UX-013](#ux-013)). A default new bill's reminder can never fire ([UX-032](#ux-032)). The app lock isn't applied until a restart and fails open ([UX-011](#ux-011), [UX-012](#ux-012)). Overdue bills drop off Home ([UX-006](#ux-006)).

<a id="ux-001"></a>
#### UX-001 · Home and the widget keep yesterday's figures after midnight or an overnight resume

**Screen:** Home (hero, Spent today, Day X of N, greeting); home-screen widget · **Severity:** Critical · **Frequency:** daily, for anyone whose app stays in memory overnight · **Effort:** S · **Confidence:** Confirmed (code). How long the OS keeps the process alive needs device testing.

- **Evidence:**
  - Nothing in `lib/` reacts to resume or a date change. The only `WidgetsBindingObserver` is the lock gate (`lib/core/biometric/biometric_gate_screen.dart:38, 58-66`).
  - `DashboardBloc` reads the clock only inside a load (`lib/features/dashboard/presentation/bloc/dashboard_bloc.dart:151-152`) and reloads only on refresh-bus events (`:57-73`).
  - The greeting reads `DateTime.now` only on rebuild (`widgets/dashboard_header.dart:47`).
  - The widget payload refreshes only at cold start and on bus events (`lib/main.dart:82, 132-141`, `widget_refresh_listener.dart:42-56`). Its native "Tap to update" stale state therefore persists after the tap.
- **Repro:** open Home in the evening and background the app without killing it. After midnight, return. Home still shows yesterday's Today's Safe Spending, Spent today, "Day N" and "Good evening · {yesterday}" until a pull-to-refresh or a data change.
- **User impact:** the most important number is wrong at the moment it is used most, the morning check. "Spent today" includes yesterday's purchases, so users under-spend or decide the app is broken. Adding an expense then makes every figure jump.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Add an `AppLifecycleListener` (in `AppShell` or `main.dart`). On `resumed`, compare today's date with the date of the last dashboard load. When it differs, dispatch `DashboardRefresh` and `HomeWidgetService.updateWidgetData()`.
  - Optionally schedule a timer to the next midnight while the app is in the foreground.
  - This is bloc wiring only; no calculation changes.
- **Tests:** add a bloc or widget test with an injectable clock that advances a day. `DashboardBloc` already takes `clock`.

## D. User journey analysis

### 1. First launch and setup

**Today:** Welcome → Name → Currency (detected from the locale) → Amount → Start (today) → End (+30 days) → Confirm with a first-day preview → Home. That is 7 taps and 2 text entries, and nothing is saved until "Create budget". The steps are short, back works, and the preview runs the real engine.

**Friction:**
- The OS notification prompt and possibly the update dialog appear on cold start, before the user knows what reminders are for (UX-013, UX-026).
- The preview figure differs from the first Home figure whenever the start date isn't today (UX-030).
- The chosen currency is not saved, so the next budget defaults to INR (UX-016).
- Moving the start date later silently shortens the period (UX-044).
- Very large amounts crash the confirmation step (UX-043).
- A returning user has no way to restore a backup (UX-042).

**Proposed:**
- Keep the 7 steps.
- Defer the notification prompt to a priming card on Home after the first load.
- Persist the currency as the default.
- Base the preview on `max(today, start)`, with copy for each case.
- Keep the period length when the start moves.
- Add "Restore from a backup" on Welcome.

**Net effect:** same tap count, no surprises, and the first number Home shows matches the promise.

### 2. Add an expense and understand what it changed

**Today:** Home FAB → amount → category → "Add ₹250": 6 taps. The sheet names the budget. The snackbar says "Expense added". The hero updates underneath, but only Home shows it; from Expenses, Reports or the widget the user sees nothing.

**Friction:**
- There is no default category, so the minimum is always 6 taps.
- Back or swipe discards the entry silently (UX-023).
- If the period has ended, the error appears only after "Add" (UX-020).
- No figure and no Undo after adding (UX-025).
- The widget opens the full form, whose Save sits behind the keyboard (UX-036, UX-014).

**Proposed:**
- Snackbar: "₹250 added · ₹627 left today · Undo", using the engine's figures.
- Guard a dirty sheet against dismissal.
- Show a not-running notice in the sheet with "Start next budget".
- Open quick add from the widget.
- Optionally pre-select the most-used category; this would make it 5 taps, but it's a preference.

### 3. Morning check: "What can I spend today?"

**Today:** open the app and land on Home.
- The hero shows the amount, status, spent and left today, and the reason.
- "How it's worked out" opens the full working.
- The budget name is the title.

**Friction:**
- On a warm resume the figures can be yesterday's (UX-001).
- On a cold launch while a newer release exists, a modal update dialog comes first (UX-026, seen on the device).
- "Day 10 of 31 · 22 days left" adds up to 32 (UX-047).
- An overdue bill isn't in "Coming up" (UX-006).
- The at-risk sentence's "average" differs from Reports' "Daily average" (UX-027).
- Screen-reader users get the header merged with an unrelated subtitle (UX-045), and OMR users hear no currency (UX-046).

**Proposed:**
- Refresh on resume when the date has changed.
- Make the update prompt non-modal or once a day.
- Fix the day-count copy.
- List overdue bills first in "Coming up".
- Label each average.
- Fix the header semantics and spoken currency.

**Net effect:** the screen already answers the question well; these fixes make the answer trustworthy every morning.

### 4. Period end and managing more than one budget

**Today:** the day after October ends:
- Home shows "This budget ended 31 Oct" with "Switch budget" / "New budget". This is good.
- Budgets still shows October with a green "Active" chip.
- The only quick renewal is Settings › "Start new budget period", which always gives 31 days from today.
- Duplicate copies October's dates.
- Creating November from the form makes it active right away, even when created on 25 Oct, so Home loses October's figure for six days.
- Quick add errors after "Add".
- In Reports, "Last Month" looks empty once November is active.

**Friction:** UX-015, UX-017, UX-020, UX-021, UX-022, UX-035.

**Proposed:**
- One "Start next period" action on Home's ended card, on budget details and on the ended active card. Prefill start = old end + 1 day, the same length, amount, currency and set-asides, then make it active.
- New budgets activate only if they're running today.
- The Active chip shows the phase.
- Reports' empty state offers "Switch budget".

### 5. Reviewing last month's spending

**Today:** Reports › "Last Month" shows the active budget's expenses only, under a calendar-month label. Export PDF uses ₹ and Helvetica.

**Friction:**
- An empty or partial month when last month was a different budget (UX-017).
- The scope isn't stated.
- Filters are almost invisible (UX-053).
- The PDF has the wrong currency or boxes (UX-010).
- Bucket labels don't match the range (UX-052).
- Chart values can't be reached by screen reader (UX-049).
- Export fails on iPad (UX-019).
